# Kima — design context

Written 2026-08-18 via `/impeccable teach`. Load this before any design pass
(`/layout`, `/adapt`, `/animate`, `/polish`, `/typeset`, `/critique`).

## Design Context

### Users

People who own their music and refuse to rent it. They rip, collect and tag a
library, run Kima on their own hardware in Docker, and point it at that
collection. They are comfortable self-hosting — most arrive already running
Lidarr, Audiobookshelf, or a Subsonic client, and Kima integrates with all
three. This is a public, GPL-v3 product distributed on Docker Hub, so the
audience is other people's servers, not one person's laptop.

Two contexts, both of them real:

- **Installed as a PWA on a phone**, one hand, often in the dark, often while
  something is already playing. This is the harder case and it is the one that
  gets checked first.
- **On a desktop**, browsing a catalogue that may run to tens of thousands of
  rows, where the job is finding something specific rather than being served
  something new.

The job is *retrieval and ownership*, not discovery-as-entertainment. Nobody is
here to be fed a feed. They are here to reach their own shelf.

### Brand Personality

**Quiet, exacting, yours.**

The music is the star and the interface gets out of its way. Restraint is the
point: amber is used sparingly enough that it still means something, space does
the work that borders and stamps used to do, and nothing shouts for attention it
has not earned.

"Exacting" is about care, not coldness — the feeling of a collection that has
been catalogued properly by someone who minded. The emotional target is calm
confidence: you can find the thing, and the thing is yours.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark only**, and deliberately so. A music player used with headphones at night
has no business being white. The codebase commits to this and there is no
light-mode plan.

**Familiar bones, own character.** Kima keeps the conventions people already
know — the player bar, the media grid, the queue — so nothing has to be learned.
The surface on top of those bones should be unmistakably not Spotify. Adopt the
patterns; do not adopt the look.

**Anti-references**, all of them things this interface has actually been:

- The brutalist-terminal dialect: monospace, capitals and bold used as texture
  rather than meaning. Removed 2026-08-18; do not let it back in.
- Hot pink (`#ec4899`) as an accent. Retired across 31 colour values.
- Decoration standing in for hierarchy — shimmer sweeps, per-item gradients,
  scale-on-hover-and-active, frosted glass behind ordinary controls.
- Spotify's actual visual language. Its patterns yes, its skin no.

### Design Principles

1. **Hierarchy comes from scale, weight and colour — never from stamping.**
   Capitals and monospace are not texture. Each has exactly one job (see the
   type system below) and no other use is legitimate. If something needs to feel
   more important, make it bigger, heavier or brighter.

2. **Every piece of text is legible or it is not shipped.** WCAG AA against the
   element's *actual* background, not the page ground — most labels live inside
   a card that is lighter than the canvas. Three text tones exist; use them.

3. **The phone is the hard case, so it is the first case.** Touch targets are
   44px. Nothing overflows horizontally at 388px. Adapt the interface for the
   smaller screen rather than amputating features from it.

4. **One pattern, one implementation.** Kima has repeatedly grown three
   divergent copies of the same thing — three paginations, two identical filter
   pill rows, four overlapping loading components. When you find the second
   copy, extract the primitive instead of styling both.

5. **Restraint is the brand.** Before adding a visual effect, ask what it tells
   the user. Motion that conveys a state change earns its place; motion that
   decorates does not. The accent is powerful because it is rare.

## Constraints

- **Stack**: Next.js 16 (Turbopack), React, Tailwind CSS v4 with `@theme`.
  Design tokens live in `app/globals.css`.
- **Fonts are self-hosted**, not fetched from a CDN — the app must work offline
  and must not phone home. Any new face ships as a woff2 in `public/fonts/` and
  loads through `next/font/local`.
- **PWA**: installed to the home screen on iOS and Android, and expected to work
  offline via a service worker.
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA contrast, 44px touch targets, keyboard focus rings,
  and `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured — including in JavaScript-driven motion,
  which a CSS media query cannot reach.
- **Density**: a little airier than today, particularly between sections.
  Spacing currently carries no rhythm, so nothing signals where one thing ends
  and the next begins.

## The type system (established 2026-08-18 — do not undo)

Six roles in `app/globals.css`. They replaced a system where 246 elements were
set in capitals, 399 lines in a monospace face used nowhere else in the product,
and bold sat on 246 of 320 weighted elements — nearly all at 10px or 12px.

| role | job |
| --- | --- |
| `.t-eyebrow` | The short status kicker above a page title. **The only capitals in the app.** |
| `.t-label` | Names a group, a column or a field. Never a sentence. |
| `.t-meta` | Counts, durations, track numbers, dates. Digits align via tabular figures. |
| `.t-body` | Sentences: descriptions, help text, empty-state copy. |
| `.t-control` | Anything you press: buttons, tabs, links. |
| `.t-data` / `code` | Machine strings only — pairing codes, API tokens, server URLs. **The only monospace in the app.** |

Three text tones, measured against the `#0a0a0a` ground:

- `--text-primary` `#e5e5e5` — 16.4:1 — titles, values, emphasis
- `--text-secondary` `#a3a3a3` — 8.6:1 — sentences, descriptions
- `--text-muted` `#858585` — 5.4:1 on the canvas, 4.7:1 inside a card — labels, meta

Brand amber is `--color-brand` `#fca200`.

**Two rules that keep being rediscovered the hard way.** Capitals run about 15%
wider than sentence case and discard the word-shape the eye reads by, which is
how "Lesser-played gems" reached a phone as "LESSER-PLAY…". And numbers want
their digits to line up, which is what `tabular-nums` is for — it never required
a second typeface.

## Typography direction

Montserrat stays for body and UI. It is already shipped, self-hosted and
regression-free, and it is a perfectly competent geometric sans.

**No display face, no stacked titles.** A previous pass split page titles
across two lines with a two-tone colour break ("Play / lists", "Good /
afternoon") and recorded a plan to set them in Archivo Expanded. The
operator rejected that pattern outright on 2026-08-20 -- it was never
wanted -- and all four stacked titles were collapsed to ordinary
single-line page headings. Page titles are Montserrat, white, one line.
Do not reintroduce stacked or split-title treatments.

## The motion system (established 2026-08-18)

Motion is CSS-driven throughout. There is no animation library and no
JavaScript render loop outside the vibe scene, which runs react-three-fiber on
`frameloop="demand"` and therefore only draws on interaction.

Easing and duration are set once, as Tailwind defaults, so every transition in
the app inherits them rather than each site picking its own:

- `--default-transition-timing-function` is `ease-out-quart`
  (`cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1)`). Before this, 540 transitions ran on the
  CSS default `ease`, which accelerates and decelerates equally — nothing
  physical moves that way, and it is why state changes felt mechanical.
- Durations are named for their job: `--duration-feedback` 120ms,
  `--duration-state` 200ms, `--duration-layout` 320ms, `--duration-entrance`
  480ms. Exits run at roughly three quarters of their entrance.
- No bounce or elastic curve is offered. They overshoot, which draws the eye to
  the animation instead of to the thing that changed.

`prefers-reduced-motion` zeroes duration **and delay**, and disables smooth
scrolling. Zeroing duration alone was worse than doing nothing on a staggered
reveal: the animation became instant while the per-row delay stayed, so the
list popped in one row at a time on a timer. The preference is also published
as `--motion-ok` (1 or 0) for any JavaScript that needs to read it, alongside
the `useReducedMotion` hook.

**Press feedback is the touch counterpart of hover.** The app has 888 hover
states and, before this pass, 23 press states — so a pointer was told something
had happened 888 times and a finger 23 times, because `hover:` never fires for
touch. Every control now dips opacity on `:active` under `(hover: none)`,
instantly on the way down and eased on the way back up.

### Two things that turned out not to be true

Recorded because they were written down as findings and acted on before being
checked:

- `transition-all` (239 uses) does **not** animate layout properties here. The
  hover states it pairs with are scale, shadow, border, background, text and
  opacity — all paint or composite. It is a latent hazard if someone later
  animates a width on one of those elements, not a live source of jank.
- The three `requestAnimationFrame` call sites are **not** unguarded
  animations. Two are not animation at all — one defers a state flip by a
  frame so a CSS transition can run, the other throttles a scroll handler —
  and the third throttles pointer-locked mouse-look, which is direct input and
  would break if it were suppressed.

## Open work

- `/polish` — alignment and micro-detail. The last pass.
- Neutral drop shadows (101 of them) do not encode elevation: `shadow-lg` and
  `shadow-2xl` sit side by side in the same files. On a `#0a0a0a` ground a drop
  shadow does little that the surface-lightness scale is not already doing, so
  the honest fix is probably to delete the system rather than restyle it.
- Two stacking-order oddities, recorded in the z-index token block and
  deliberately not changed: the fixed top bar and in-page dropdowns share tier
  50, and the activity panel (100) sits above modals (80), so a dialog opened
  while the panel is out should render behind it.
- A touch device at 769–1366px has not been verified end to end; Chrome's
  emulated viewport does not follow a window resize.
